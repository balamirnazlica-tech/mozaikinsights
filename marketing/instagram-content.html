Instagram Content Performance

Every post from 1 August to 31 October 2026 — 81 posts, classified by what kind of content it is. Built to answer one question: what actually drives reach?

The month that confused everyone

September's organic reach (67,489) looked like a breakthrough and October's (35,393) looked like a collapse. Neither is true. One post made September, and the content mix changed underneath it.

Reach by content type

Mean reach per post. Project posts — a finished interior, crediting the architecture studio and tagging the brands — are the strongest category in all three months. Single-product design-history posts are the weakest, in both months they ran.

What each month was made of

Share of posts published, by type. September is the only month with no product-education posts — that alone lifts its average.

Posting more did not buy more reach

October published 55% more posts than September for 7% less total post reach, and unique organic reach fell. Meanwhile reach to your own followers rose 37%. The extra posts were shown to the same people.

Format matters less than content

Carousels out-reach Reels on average, but the best Reel of October was a project post (Fiko Etiler, 6,676). Content type beats format — a project post works in any format.

What to do

  1. Make project posts the backbone — two or three a week. Best category in August, September and October. You published six in October. You have the archive and they cost nothing to produce. This is the biggest single lever.
  2. Cut the single-product design-history posts, or stop counting them as reach. 13 in August, 14 in October, averaging 3,623 and 3,068. They are roughly 40% of output for the weakest return. Keep them for brand depth if you want — just don't let them fill the calendar.
  3. Start using Instagram Collab. Checked all 81 posts: zero used the Collab field. When an influencer or partner posts on their own account you get none of that audience. A Collab post sits natively in both feeds. For the next shoot this is free distribution you are currently throwing away.
  4. Tag harder on project posts. The best ones credit the studio and tag six to eight brands. LEGASEA (2 Oct) tagged one brand and reached 2,424 — the weakest project post of the quarter.
  5. Judge the editorial campaign on something other than reach. The Büyükada production — models, agencies, full crew — averaged 5,541 across five September posts and 4,739 across three in October. Respectable, not exceptional, and far more expensive than a project post.
Read this before quoting the numbers. Post-level reach includes paid delivery when a post was boosted, so these figures measure total reach, not organic only. Comparisons between months, content types and formats hold; the absolute values are not organic reach. They also do not sum to the monthly organic figure on the KPI tracker — Meta de-duplicates people across posts, so summing post reach counts the same person many times. The monthly organic/paid split comes from Meta Business Suite, which is the only place Meta reports it, and is entered by hand.
